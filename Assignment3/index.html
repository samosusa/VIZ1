The Boyband Blueprint

Each generation of boybands had its own look

This visualization shows 234 members of 55 boybands. The bands go clockwise around the circle, from the oldest generation to the youngest, sorted by the average birth year of their members. Each spoke represents one member and shows his hair. In this dataset, members born in the 1970s mostly have short hair and account for half of the 14 frosted-tip records. Members born in the 1990s mostly have medium-length hair, and all red, blue, green and silver hair-colour records belong to this group.

How to read this visualization

Where is the band?

Bands are ordered clockwise by their average available birth year, from oldest to youngest. Arc length shows the number of members. The outer ring groups bands by the decade of their average birth year. Angular distances do not represent elapsed years.

Click a band to explore its members, or use Tab to focus a band and Enter or Space to select it.

How long is his hair?

A dashed short spoke means the length is unknown.

      Reflection

      Visualization question

      • My visualization investigates how the boyband look (hair) changes between generations of boyband members.
      • The most important data attributes are band, date of birth, hair colour, hair length and frosted tips.
      • The reader should first notice the generations around the circle and how the spokes get shorter for the 1970s and longer again for the 1990s.
      • The reader should then be able to explore single bands and their members, and where a given hair colour appears.

      Marks and channels

      Data attributeMarkChannelWhy this choice?

      Average birth year (quantitative, derived)ArcAngle (order around the circle)The circle reads like a clock, so going clockwise means going forward in time.
      Number of members (quantitative, derived)ArcArc lengthBig bands take more of the circle, and every member gets the same amount of space.
      Birth decade (ordinal, derived)Thin outer arc + labelSpatial regionGroups the bands into generations so the reader can compare them.
      Hair length (ordinal)Line (spoke)LengthLength is a strong channel for ordered data, and a longer line feels like longer hair.
      Hair colour (categorical)Point (circle)Colour hueHue is good for categories, and the real hair colours need no lookup.
      Frosted tips (categorical)Point (diamond)Shape + hueA different shape pops out from the circles, and it is only drawn when there are frosted tips.

      What does the arc structure represent?

      The ring orders bands by their average available birth year. Angular distances do not represent elapsed time. d3.pie() splits the circle into one arc per band, with arc length representing the number of members. A thin outer ring groups bands by the decade of their average available birth year. SVG rotations position the member spokes inside their band's arc. Math.sin() and Math.cos() position the decade labels. A small gap at 12 o'clock marks the beginning and end of the sequence.

      Attributes I chose not to visualize

      Height is missing for 115 of 234 members, so drawing it would mostly show missing data. Clothes, accessories, eye colour and instruments are interesting, but adding them would bury the message about hair and generations. Skin colour was left out on purpose: the question is about style choices, not about the members' bodies.

      Interaction

      Clicking a band highlights it, dims the others and opens a panel with the members' names, birth dates and hair. This gives the overview first and the details on demand. Clicking a hair colour in the legend dims all other members, so the reader can see where that colour appears across the generations (for example blonde or the dyed colours). Both use short opacity transitions so the reader can follow the change. Band arcs and hair-colour buttons support keyboard activation. Selected-band details are presented as HTML in a polite live region.

      What I would improve with more time

      • The six bands with no birth dates sit in an "unknown" section. Finding their birth years would complete the timeline.
      • Some people appear twice (for example Ricky Bell in New Edition and Bell Biv DeVoe, and IMx is a renamed Immature). I would mark those links.
      • Test the hair colours with a colour-blindness simulator and with real users.
